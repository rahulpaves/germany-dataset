The problem

Advising one student on Germany means opening fifteen tabs: the programme page for entry requirements, uni-assist to work out whether their board is even recognised, a PDF for the English threshold, and the local German mission's site for a visa process that differs by nationality.

Then the deadline moves, the blocked-account figure changes for the new year, or a programme switches from German to English — and the notes you built last cycle are quietly wrong.

Multiply that by every student asking about Germany, and the research is costing you more hours than the counselling.

Entry requirements

  • General: A recognised higher-education entrance qualification (Hochschulzugangsberechtigung).
  • Equivalence: Non-German qualifications are assessed by uni-assist, which issues the preliminary review documentation (VPD) TUM requires before it will review the file.
  • Selection: Aptitude Assessment (Eignungsfeststellungsverfahren) — competitive, and the stage most applicants underestimate.
  • English: At least B2. Proficiency is normally verified as part of the aptitude assessment rather than by a fixed IELTS or TOEFL score.
  • Pre-study internship: Minimum eight weeks, ideally documented by 31 October of the first year of study.
  • India & China: An APS certificate is required. Allow 4–8 weeks and start it before the application, not after.

Deadline & how to apply

15 May – 15 July 2027 Winter semester 2027/28 · the 2026/27 window closed on 15 July 2026
  • Step 1 — Indian and Chinese applicants: begin the APS certificate first (4–8 weeks).
  • Step 2 — Submit certificates to uni-assist for the VPD. Allow several weeks; this is the step that quietly consumes the application window.
  • Step 3 — Apply through TUMonline within the 15 May – 15 July window.
  • Step 4 — Complete the aptitude assessment if shortlisted after the document stage.
  • Step 5 — Receive the admission decision (Zulassungsbescheid) — nothing in the visa chain can begin without it.

Student visa process — non-EU applicant

  1. 1 APS certificate 4–8 weeks

    Academic Evaluation Centre verification. Required for the visa and commonly for admission too, so it belongs at the very start of the timeline.

  2. 2 Admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid) after the July deadline

    Conditional offers are not accepted for the visa file — it must be the formal admission decision.

  3. 3 Open a blocked account (Sperrkonto) 1–2 weeks

    EUR 11,904 for one year, released at EUR 992 per month. Unchanged for 2026, but revised annually — always re-check. A parental guarantee (Verpflichtungserklärung) is the main alternative.

  4. 4 Arrange German health insurance 3–7 days

    Public insurer confirmation (TK, AOK or similar) for enrolled students, plus travel cover for the arrival gap.

  5. 5 Book the visa appointment 2–12 weeks wait

    The real bottleneck. Availability at the German mission varies sharply by country and season — book the moment admission lands, not once documents are complete.

  6. 6 Submit the national visa (Type D) application appointment day

    Passport, admission letter, blocked-account confirmation, insurance, transcripts, CV, motivation letter, biometric photos, and the APS certificate where it applies. Fee EUR 75.

  7. 7 Visa processing 4–8 weeks

    End to end — APS, admission, appointment and processing — the realistic span is three to six months. That is why a July deadline becomes a tight October start.

  8. 8 Arrive and register (Anmeldung) within 2 weeks of arrival

    City registration at the Bürgeramt, requiring a signed accommodation confirmation from the landlord.

  9. 9 Convert to a residence permit before the visa expires

    Apply at the Ausländerbehörde for the student residence permit (Aufenthaltserlaubnis) — before the visa expires, or within 90 days of arrival if your student entered visa-free.

Same programme — four different paths, by nationality

The timeline above is the standard non-EU route. Which steps actually apply depends entirely on the passport your student holds.

No visa, no permit
EU / EEA / Switzerland

All EU member states, plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland.

Freedom of movement applies. No visa, no residence permit, no blocked account. Enrol, then register the address (Anmeldung) within two weeks of arrival. That is the entire process.

Visa-free entry, permit obtained in Germany
The privileged eight

Australia, Canada, Israel, Japan, New Zealand, Republic of Korea, United Kingdom, United States.

Your student may enter Germany without a visa and apply for the residence permit at the local Ausländerbehörde within the first 90 days. This removes the embassy appointment entirely — which is the single biggest bottleneck in the standard route. Financial proof and health insurance are still required, just presented in Germany rather than beforehand.

Type D visa + APS
APS countries

India, China, Vietnam and Mongolia.

The full Type D chain, preceded by an APS certificate from the Academic Evaluation Centre — without it there is no student visa. Budget four weeks to three months, and roughly INR 18,000 in India. New for Summer Semester 2027: Indian Master's applicants coming from Engineering, Commerce, Accounting, Finance, Economics, Business or Management must also submit a dMAT certificate alongside APS.

Type D visa, no APS
All other non-EU nationalities

The standard nine-step route above, without the APS stage. The variable that matters most is appointment availability at your student's local German mission, which swings from a fortnight to three months by country and season.
